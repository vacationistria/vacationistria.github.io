Your Starting Point

Boat Excursions in Pula, Istria – Islands, Caves & Dolphin Tours

Most visitors to Pula discover the city on foot – the Roman amphitheatre, the old town, the seafront promenade. But the people who leave with the strongest memories are the ones who stepped onto a boat.

From the water, Pula looks completely different. The coastline opens up, the islands appear, and suddenly you understand why this corner of the Adriatic has drawn travellers for centuries. Boat excursions in Pula are not just a leisure activity – they are the most direct way to experience what southern Istria is actually made of: clear sea, wild islands, unhurried nature, and a pace of life that slows the moment you leave the harbour.

This guide covers every type of boat excursion available from Pula – what to expect, how to choose, what things cost, and which experience is right for you. Whether this is your first visit to Istria or your fifth, the sea around Pula always has something new to show.

The Destination

Brijuni Islands – The Heart of Every Boat Excursion

Ask anyone who has taken a boat excursion from Pula what they remember most, and the answer is almost always Brijuni. The Brijuni archipelago sits just a few kilometres off the coast – close enough to feel within reach, far enough from the mainland to feel genuinely removed from everyday life.

Fourteen islands make up the national park. The sea around them changes colour depending on the depth and the angle of the sun – deep blue in open water, shifting to a pale turquoise in the shallower channels between islands. From the deck of a boat, the coastline of Brijuni reveals something that no photograph quite captures: the silence, the scale, and the particular quality of light that this part of the Adriatic holds in summer.

The islands carry layers of history that most visitors don’t expect. Roman ruins are still visible along the shore. For decades Brijuni served as the private retreat of Josip Broz Tito, who hosted heads of state here and kept a personal zoo on the main island – gifts from visiting dignitaries. Today the park is open to everyone, and that history gives the excursion a dimension beyond scenery alone.

The two types of excursion are different enough that they don’t really compete – they serve different purposes and attract different kinds of travellers. Boat excursions in Pula typically last between 3 and 5 hours, depending on the type of experience. Day trips tend to focus on island visits and swimming stops, while evening excursions lean toward dolphin watching and sunset views over the open Adriatic. Exact itineraries, departure times, and what is included vary by operator – so it is always worth checking the details before booking.

Good to Know

What Is the Difference Between Boat Tours and Boat Excursions in Pula?

Travelers often use these two terms interchangeably – and on most booking platforms you will find them mixed together without distinction. In practice, the difference is mostly about structure and flexibility:

TermWhat it typically meansBest for
Boat TourAn organized, structured trip with a fixed itinerary, scheduled departure times, a guide, and set inclusions like food and drinks.First-time visitors who want a hassle-free, complete experience
Boat ExcursionA broader term covering all types of trips – group tours, private charters, themed experiences, and shorter cruises.Travelers who want flexibility, privacy, or a custom itinerary

For most visitors to Pula, the distinction matters very little in practice. The important questions are simpler: How long is the trip? What is included? Do you want to swim, watch dolphins, or both? Once you know that, finding the right boat tour in Pula becomes straightforward.

Before You Go

Tips Before Booking a Boat Excursion in Pula

Small practical details make a bigger difference than most people expect. These are the things experienced Adriatic travellers know – and first-timers sometimes wish they had known sooner:

  • Book early in July and August. Pula is busy in high season and the better excursions fill up several days in advance. Waiting until the morning of departure is a risk – especially for groups or families who need multiple seats together.
  • Evening is better for dolphins. Dolphin sightings happen on both departures, but the frequency is significantly higher in the evening. If watching dolphins in open water is important to you, the sunset tour is the right choice.
  • The midday sun is stronger than it looks. On the water, reflected UV adds up quickly. Bring high-factor sunscreen, a hat you can actually wear on a moving boat, and a towel – even if you don’t plan to swim.
  • Bring your own snorkeling gear for Jerolim. The water around St. Jerolim Island is clear enough to make snorkeling genuinely worthwhile. The boat doesn’t provide equipment, so if you have your own – bring it.
  • Arrive 30 minutes before departure. The boat leaves from the Riva waterfront next to ACI Marina Pula. Arriving early means you get to choose your seat, settle in with a drink, and start the experience calmly rather than rushing aboard at the last second.
  • Parking near the Colosseum is your best option. If you are coming by car, the paid parking near the Pula Arena is the most practical choice – a short, flat walk from the departure point on the Riva.
Plan Your Trip

Best Time for Boat Excursions in Pula

Boat excursions in Pula run from late spring through early autumn – typically May to October – with the peak season falling in July and August. Each period has its own character, and the “best” time depends on what kind of experience you are after.

June is widely considered the sweet spot. The sea is warm enough for comfortable swimming, the crowds are lighter than in midsummer, and the dolphins are reliably active. Prices tend to be slightly lower than in peak season, and boats rarely sell out days in advance.

July and August are the busiest months. The weather is at its most reliable, the atmosphere on board is at its most social, and sunset tours in particular fill up quickly. Booking several days ahead is strongly recommended during this period.

September is an underrated choice. The sea remains warm from the summer months, tourist numbers drop noticeably, and the light during golden-hour tours is arguably the best of the year. Many experienced Adriatic travellers consider September the most enjoyable time to be on the water around Pula.

May and October offer quiet conditions and lower prices, though water temperatures are cooler and not all operators run daily departures. Worth checking availability in advance if you are visiting during these months.

Common Questions

FAQ – Frequently Asked Questions

These are the questions guests ask most often before booking. Answered clearly, without filler:

How long does the boat excursion in Pula last?

Most boat excursions in Pula last between 3 and 5 hours. Daytime trips with island and swimming stops tend to run longer, while evening sunset cruises are typically shorter. Exact departure times and durations vary by operator, so it is worth checking the specific details when booking.

What is included in the Boat Excursion in Pula?

A standard group excursion ticket typically includes:

  • One meal (fish, burger, or vegetarian option)
  • Unlimited drinks (wine, soft drinks, mineral water)
  • Professional and friendly crew
  • Scenic cruise around Brijuni Islands
  • Music and relaxed atmosphere on board

For exact pricing, visit the boat tour page.

Does the excursion include swimming?

Yes – daytime excursions typically include a swimming stop at St. Jerolim Island, part of Brijuni National Park, with crystal-clear water and a beautiful beach. Bringing your own snorkeling equipment is recommended. Evening excursions generally focus on dolphin watching and do not include a swimming stop. Always check with your operator before booking.

Is there an entrance fee for St. Jerolim?

Yes. St. Jerolim is part of Brijuni National Park, so there is a small entrance fee paid on site: approximately 7€ for adults and 5€ for children. This applies only to guests on the daytime tour. There is no extra park fee on the evening excursion.

Will we really see dolphins?

Dolphins are spotted on the vast majority of evening sunset tours. While wildlife sightings can never be 100% guaranteed, the Pula coastline is one of the best places in the Adriatic to see dolphins in their natural habitat. During the day, sightings are less frequent – the evening departure is strongly recommended if dolphins are a priority for you.

Are children allowed on board?

Absolutely. The tours are fully family-friendly. Children of all ages are welcome on both the daytime and evening excursions. Children under 3 years of age travel completely free of charge.

Are pets allowed on board?

Yes! Pets are allowed on both daytime and evening excursions. The boat has plenty of space and the crew is happy to welcome your four-legged companion on board.

Where is the departure point?

The boat departs from the Riva waterfront in Pula, right next to the ACI Marina Pula. The Riva is easy to find and accessible from the city center on foot. If arriving by car, the most convenient parking is near the Pula Colosseum (Arena), a short walk from the departure point. Plan to arrive at least 30 minutes before departure.

Is the excursion suitable for groups and celebrations?

Absolutely. Boat excursions in Pula are ideal for:

  • Birthday celebrations at sea
  • Summer parties and group vacations
  • Team building events and corporate gatherings
  • Romantic anniversary cruises

The relaxed, social atmosphere on board often turns into a real party – especially on the evening tour.

How many people are on a boat excursion in Pula?

Group excursions in Pula typically accommodate between 30 and 60 guests depending on the operator and vessel. The atmosphere is relaxed rather than crowded – there is always enough space to sit comfortably, move around, and enjoy the views. If you prefer a more exclusive experience with full flexibility over the itinerary, a private boat charter is also available on request.
